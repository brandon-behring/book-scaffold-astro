# Roadmap — `book-scaffold-astro`

**Original date:** 2026-06-14 · **Refreshed:** 2026-07-13 · **Original baseline:** v4.24.0 · **Published baseline:** v5.2.0 · **Status:** active

This is the post-reconciliation refresh tracked by #185. It preserves the
original roadmap's consumer-signal and design-before-code reasoning, while
absorbing the tracker work filed through #212, the v4.26 release repair, the
v4.27 stabilization batch, the production-verified #187 performance fix, the
four post-stabilization slices (#143, #147, #190, and #161/#164), the v5.0.0
corpus/configuration tranche, the v5.1.0 readiness release, and the v5.2.0
per-page social-card release. No P1/P2 implementation slice remains selected;
open work is either P3 rollout/watch or explicitly parked on a trigger.

Status terms in this document are deliberate:

- **Shipped** means the change is on `main`, tagged, and published in both
  lock-step npm packages.
- **In flight** means the contract is selected and implementation exists or is
  under review, but it is not part of a published artifact yet.
- **Rollout / watch** means lower-priority cross-repository or portfolio work
  without a committed release.
- **Parked / on signal** means intentionally unscheduled until its recorded
  trigger fires; it is not forgotten work.

## v5.0 corpus gate — shipped

### Direction retained: corpus as monorepo (#80)

The independent-consumer trigger fired: `guides-ai-engineering` joined the
DLAI study-notes pilot as a second single-app, multi-guide consumer. The chosen
direction remains **one app, one build, multiple namespaced books**. It
graduates the way both consumers already operate instead of asking them to
federate independent deployments.

The decision-complete contracts are recorded in
[`v5-corpus-contract.md`](v5-corpus-contract.md),
[`qa-contract.md`](qa-contract.md), and
[`og-card-contract.md`](og-card-contract.md). The corpus contract preserves
Recipe 21's `/chapters/<book>/<slug>/` public URLs, selects one homogeneous
preset/Style chain per Astro app, derives book identity from entry ids, and
defines the exact route, artifact, diagnostic, Pagefind, and migration model.
The v5.0 implementation follows the corpus contract, v5.1.0 implements the QA
contract, and v5.2.0 implements the OG-card contract.

The cost model from the original roadmap still matters:

- Route injection is centralized in `ROUTE_REGISTRY`; adding book-aware route
  patterns is not principally a dispatcher rewrite.
- Profile schemas are selected independently; this is not principally a Zod
  union rewrite.
- The expensive surface is book-scoping the build products and validation:
  labels/counters, exercises, tips, references, route parameters, links,
  readiness counts, and search/navigation across a shared corpus.
- Federation remains an unfired, separate need. It would require a corpus-wide
  search index, landing/selector behavior, and deployment/base-prefix rules that
  neither signaling consumer requested.

The v4.26 book-aware navigation release was a no-regret slice of #80. v5.0
completes the remaining contract by implementing:

1. the corpus/book manifest and profile-composition model;
2. route identities and `getStaticPaths` parameters for corpus landing,
   per-book chapter indexes, chapters, and apparatus routes;
3. book-scoped output keys and diagnostics for every build/validate command;
4. Pagefind indexing, result URLs, and navigation at both book and corpus level;
5. single-book compatibility and migration from recipe 21's `generateId`
   workaround; and
6. how #147, #158, and #190 compose with the corpus contract without being
   falsely treated as already implemented by it.

#158 stayed intentionally **outside** the v5.0 milestone. Its interface,
metrics, selectors, and exit codes were designed with #80, then shipped in
v5.1.0 against the completed book resolver. #157 followed independently in
v5.2.0 with per-book social-card identity.

## Sequencing guardrails

1. v5.0.0, v5.1.0, and v5.2.0 are published and verified; no P1/P2
   implementation slice is currently selected.
2. Keep P3 rollout/watch items independent; do not reopen the shipped corpus,
   readiness, or social-card gates to batch unrelated work.
3. Preserve the v4 deprecation and migration record for the two intentional
   v5 breaks.
4. Prioritize demonstrated consumer failure and shared duplication over small
   speculative features. Effort is a planning input, not the ordering rule.
5. Keep stable demo plumbing separate from volatile domain APIs.
6. Do not invent VersionSelector or Anki data contracts without a deployment or
   import workflow able to validate them.
7. Treat the accepted #80 scope and migration plan as the compatibility
   boundary; amend it explicitly before changing corpus route/tooling behavior.
