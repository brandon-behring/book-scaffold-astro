*This chapter is an exemplar, not content. Its purpose is to show the
full shape — frontmatter, section order, callouts, citations — so a
new author can copy the skeleton and replace the prose. Delete the
file once you have a real chapter 1 in place. The `draft: true`
frontmatter and leading-underscore filename keep it out of the build
while you reference it.*

## Representation

What is the thing, conceptually? This section defines the object the
chapter is about. Give its structural properties, its type signature,
the invariants that make it itself.<Sidenote>Sidenotes float to the right margin on desktop and reflow inline as colored asides on mobile. Citations resolve via `<Citation src="slug" />` to entries in `sources/manifest.yaml`; try the two example citations below.</Sidenote>

A chapter on *hooks* (to use a concrete example from agentic coding)
would define here: what a hook is as a computational object; what
distinguishes a hook from a regular script; what invariants (event
type, payload shape, timeout budget) every hook carries.

<ConceptBox term="Koller-Friedman skeleton">
Three sections per chapter, always in this order: Representation
(what is it?), Operation (how does it behave?), Evolution (how does
it change over time?). The uniform shape defends against drift and
makes the book legible at a glance. See the scaffold skill's
`pedagogy/kf-chapter-shape.md` for the full argument.
</ConceptBox>

## Operation

How does the thing behave? Show the dynamics — how it's used, how it
fails, how it composes. Include the verification or testing
perspective. A well-formed Operation section leaves the reader able
to recognize the object in the wild and not surprised by its failure
modes.

<SkillBox title="Audit your frontmatter before writing">
Every chapter's frontmatter drives the freshness badge, tool filter,
and source archive. Before adding prose, confirm `volatility`,
`tools_compared`, and `last_verified` are honest. Wrong metadata
lies to readers about how much to trust the chapter.
</SkillBox>

## Evolution

How does the thing change over time? Show comparative and historical
evidence. This is where Convergence and Divergence callouts belong.

<Convergence tools={['claude-code', 'gemini-cli', 'codex-cli']}>
When multiple tools adopt the same pattern, the Convergence callout
signals that a reader can rely on this claim across the ecosystem
rather than hedging per-tool. Gold palette, solid left-bar.
</Convergence>

<Divergence axis="failure-mode of the example pattern">
When tools disagree on a design choice, the Divergence callout
spells out the axis explicitly. Dashed left-bar on gold signals
instability — this is still an open design space, not settled
practice.
</Divergence>

<Recovery

</Recovery>

<KeyIdea>
The KF skeleton is load-bearing. If a chapter resists the shape —
"this chapter is really only about Operation" — that's a signal the
chapter should be two chapters, or that it's documenting a
feature-surface rather than arguing a pattern. Trust the shape.
</KeyIdea>
